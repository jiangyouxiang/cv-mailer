# Application Email Composition

Use this guide while preparing the message and the user-facing review snapshot.

## Select content from evidence

Read the current resume and requested supporting files. Choose two to four achievements that match the advertised role. Prefer concrete, verifiable details such as research area, representative publications, funded or led projects, teaching coverage, professional practice, software, or methods expertise.

Keep claims proportional to the evidence. Do not convert work in progress into completed output, a journal quartile into a broader ranking claim, participation into leadership, or semantic screening into psychometric validation. Omit a questionable fact or ask the user rather than guessing.

## Recommended message structure

1. Specific subject: role + application purpose + candidate name when known.
2. Salutation using the named contact; otherwise use a neutral hiring salutation.
3. Opening paragraph: identity, current affiliation or degree status, target role, and reason for writing.
4. Fit paragraph: research or professional focus and two to four relevant achievements.
5. Contribution paragraph: teaching, practice, technical, or service value relevant to the role.
6. Link paragraph: verified academic or professional homepage when useful.
7. Closing paragraph: mention the attached resume and invite further contact.
8. Conventional closing, aligned signature, and visible attachment note.

Keep the email concise. Summarize the resume rather than reproducing it.

## Formatting

Prefer standards-compatible HTML with inline styles when supported:

- common system or CJK sans-serif font;
- approximately 14 px body text;
- 1.7–1.8 line height;
- consistent 12–16 px paragraph spacing;
- 2-em indentation for body paragraphs;
- no indentation on the salutation, closing, signature, or attachment line;
- clickable, fully qualified links;
- simple formatting that survives webmail sanitization.

Avoid decorative banners, complex tables, remote tracking images, large colored headings, or layout that depends on external CSS.

When HTML or multipart messages are unavailable, use plain text with blank lines between paragraphs and two full-width spaces at the start of Chinese body paragraphs. Keep signature fields on separate lines and include a final attachment line.

## Required review snapshot

Show this complete block in chat before sending:

```text
UNSENT — awaiting approval

From: ...
To: ...
Cc: ...
Bcc: ...
Subject: ...
Attachments:
- filename.ext (type and size when available)
Links:
- https://...

Body:
[the complete body exactly as it will be sent]
```

Do not replace the body with a summary. If HTML is used, present a readable rendering or plain-text equivalent and state any formatting that will be applied. Approval covers the exact recipients, wording, links, attachment contents, and formatting represented by this snapshot.

## Revision handling

Label revisions clearly, such as Draft v2. After any change, display the complete snapshot again. Do not rely on a diff alone, and do not carry approval forward from an earlier version.
